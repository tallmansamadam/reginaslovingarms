Choosing an agency

How to Choose a Home Care Agency in Chicago: Agency vs. Private Caregiver vs. Franchise vs. Registry

By Samuel Tallman, Chief Brand Officer Published 2026-10-17 Reviewed by: CONFIRM

Chicago has hundreds of home care providers, and they are not interchangeable. Before comparing names, compare models. Each has real trade-offs.

The four options

Option How it works Upside Watch out for
Independent local agency Locally owned company employs, screens, schedules, and supervises caregivers Direct access to owners and managers; flexibility; local relationships Quality varies widely; verify license and backup coverage
National franchise Locally owned office under a national brand (for example Comfort Keepers, Home Instead, Right at Home) Brand systems, training programs, wide recognition Pricing and quality still depend on the individual local office; published estimates for some brands run roughly $32 to $45 per hour
Private caregiver (hired directly) You hire and manage an individual Often lower hourly rate You become the employer: payroll taxes, workers' comp, scheduling, and no backup when they're sick
Registry / placement agency Matches you with an independent caregiver Choice of candidates In Illinois, registries and agencies are regulated differently; the caregiver generally isn't supervised by the registry

No model wins for everyone. A family needing a few hours a week with a trusted individual may do fine privately. A family needing reliable overnight coverage usually needs an agency with a bench of backup caregivers.

The vetting checklist

License and legal - Is the agency licensed by the Illinois Department of Public Health? Ask for the license type and number. - Are caregivers W-2 employees? That affects insurance, workers' comp, and accountability. - Does the agency run Health Care Worker Background Checks, as Illinois requires?

Reliability - What is the plan when a caregiver is sick or a no-show? - How fast can care start? (Regina's Loving Arms says many cases begin within 24 to 48 hours.) - Will you get consistent caregivers, or a rotating cast?

Money - Is pricing in writing? Are there hourly minimums per visit? Many agencies require 2 to 4 hours. - Are there holiday, overnight, or live-in rates? - Which payers are accepted: private pay, long-term care insurance, Medicaid programs, Medicare Advantage supplemental benefits?

Care quality - Who writes and updates the care plan, and how often? - Can you meet or request a specific caregiver? - How do families reach someone after hours?

Red flags

  • No license number offered, or reluctance to share it.
  • Pricing "depends" with no written rate sheet.
  • Pressure to sign long contracts before an assessment.
  • Can't explain who supervises caregivers.

FAQ

Is an agency always better than hiring privately? Not always, but an agency removes employer duties and provides backup coverage. That matters most for daily or overnight care.

How many agencies should I interview? Three is a reasonable minimum. Ask all of them the same questions.
