How Much Does Home Care Cost in Chicago in 2026?

By Samuel Tallman, Chief Brand Officer Published 2026-10-17 Reviewed by: CONFIRM

Short answer Published estimates for non-medical home care in the Chicago area range from roughly $25 to $45 per hour, depending on the source, the agency, hours booked, and level of care. The national median for a non-medical caregiver was about $35 per hour in CareScout's 2025 Cost of Care Survey.

What the data says

Source Figure What it measures
CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025 About $35/hr national median Non-medical caregiver (homemaker and home health aide now reported together)
Care.com, July 2026 $22.97/hr average starting rate in Chicago Rates reported by caregivers on its platform; not agency pricing
HomeCareAtlas About $25 to $35/hr; $31 to $43/hr in other pages Chicago-area agency estimates
The Senior List $32 to $45/hr Comfort Keepers estimate, varies by location

The spread is real. Platform rates for individuals run lower than agency rates because agencies carry payroll taxes, insurance, background checks, supervision, and backup coverage.

What a month can look like

These are illustrations using the $35 national median, not Regina's Loving Arms rates:

  • 20 hours a week: about $3,030 a month
  • 44 hours a week: about $6,670 a month
  • 24/7 care: much higher; ask about live-in or flat daily rates, which are often priced differently than hourly

What changes the price

  • Hours and minimums. Many agencies require 2 to 4 hours per visit.
  • Overnight, weekend, and holiday rates.
  • Care complexity. Memory care or two-person transfers can cost more.
  • Live-in vs. hourly.
  • Agency vs. private hire. Hiring directly can look cheaper per hour, but you take on payroll taxes, workers' comp, and finding backup coverage.

Ways to lower the out-of-pocket cost

  1. Check insurance. Long-term care policies may pay. Regina's Loving Arms lists Aetna, BCBS Illinois, Humana, Meridian Health Plan, Molina, and Personalized Options among plans it works with, and verifies coverage at no cost. Benefits vary by plan.
  2. Look at Illinois programs. See our guide to paying for home care in Illinois.
  3. Check VA benefits. The Aid and Attendance pension can help eligible veterans and surviving spouses.
  4. Start with fewer hours. Many families begin with mornings or a few days a week and scale up.
