Home Care vs. Home Health in Illinois: What's the Difference and Which Does Your Family Need?

By Samuel Tallman, Chief Brand Officer Published 2026-10-17 Reviewed by: CONFIRM: name + credential of RN or administrator who reviews this

Short answer Home health is skilled medical care ordered by a doctor, typically after a hospital stay or illness, and it is the kind Medicare can pay for. Home care is non-medical help with daily life: bathing, meals, companionship, errands, and safety. Most older adults who "just need some help at home" need home care. Many need both at different points.

The two terms, side by side

Home care (non-medical) Home health (skilled)
What it covers Personal care, meal prep, light housekeeping, companionship, transportation, overnight and live-in support Nursing, wound care, physical/occupational/speech therapy, medication management by a clinician
Who orders it You or your family decide A physician orders it
Typical length Ongoing, from a few hours a week to 24/7 Short-term, tied to a medical need
Illinois license Home Services Agency license from the Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH) Home Health Agency (or Home Nursing Agency) license from IDPH; Medicare certification if billing Medicare
Medicare Generally does not pay for non-medical care Pays for covered skilled home health
Who pays Private pay, long-term care insurance, VA benefits, some Medicaid and state programs Medicare, Medicare Advantage, private insurance

Illinois regulates these under separate license types: IDPH licenses home services agencies for non-medical care and licenses home health and home nursing agencies for skilled care. An agency's license tells you what it is legally allowed to provide.

A home health aide is not always "home health"

This trips up many families. A home health aide who comes through Medicare's home health benefit is covered only alongside skilled care such as nursing or therapy. The same type of help, arranged directly with a home care agency for ongoing daily support, is paid for another way.

How to tell which one you need

Ask what the need actually is:

  • "Mom needs help showering, cooking, and getting to appointments." Home care.
  • "Dad is home after hip surgery and needs a nurse for his incision and physical therapy." Home health.
  • "After rehab, she needs therapy for a few weeks, then someone to help daily." Home health first, then home care. Many families plan the handoff before discharge.

If you're unsure, a hospital discharge planner, your loved one's physician, or an agency's assessment can sort it out. Regina's Loving Arms offers a complimentary in-home assessment to walk through the daily routine and recommend a level of support.

Questions to ask any agency

  1. Which Illinois license(s) do you hold, and can I see the license number?
  2. Are your caregivers employees or contractors?
  3. What happens if my regular caregiver calls in sick?
  4. Which payment sources do you accept?
  5. Who supervises the care plan, and how often is it reviewed?

FAQ

Does Medicare pay for home care in Illinois? Medicare generally does not pay for non-medical, ongoing personal care. It can cover skilled home health when a doctor orders it and other conditions are met.
