Paying for care

How to Pay for Home Care in Illinois: Insurance, the Community Care Program, VA Benefits, and Private Pay

By Regina's Loving Arms editorial team Published 2026-10-17 Reviewed by: CONFIRM

Most families use more than one source. Here are the main ones.

1. Illinois Department on Aging: Community Care Program (CCP)

CCP helps older adults who might otherwise need nursing home care stay at home. It is one of Illinois's Medicaid home and community-based services waivers. Services include care coordination, adult day services, in-home service (cleaning, meal preparation, laundry, errands), and emergency home response.

Eligibility basics (check the state for current rules): - Age 60 or older - Illinois resident, and a U.S. citizen or eligible non-citizen - Non-exempt assets of $17,500 or less (home, car, and personal furnishings are exempt) - An assessed need for long-term care through the state's Determination of Need assessment

Income doesn't decide eligibility but is used to set what a participant may contribute to the cost. Apply or get help through the Illinois Department on Aging, (800) 252-8966, or ilaging.illinois.gov.

Regina's Loving Arms is connected with IDoA and offers an IDoA Senior Program. CONFIRM: exact nature of the relationship, e.g., approved CCP provider, and whether it serves all counties listed. Ask us what the program can and cannot cover for your situation.

2. Insurance

  • Long-term care insurance. Policies often pay for in-home care once benefit triggers are met. Ask your insurer about the elimination period and required documentation.
  • Health plans. Regina's Loving Arms lists Aetna, BCBS Illinois, Humana, Meridian Health Plan, Molina Healthcare, and Personalized Options. Benefits differ by plan, so we verify coverage personally at no cost.
  • Medicare. Original Medicare does not pay for ongoing non-medical personal care. It covers skilled home health ordered by a doctor. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer limited in-home support as a supplemental benefit.

3. VA benefits

The Aid and Attendance pension can add monthly funds for eligible veterans and surviving spouses who need help with daily activities. The VA also has homemaker and home health aide programs. Contact the VA or an accredited veterans service officer for eligibility.

4. Private pay

Many families pay privately, at least in part. Ask for a written rate sheet and confirm hourly minimums and overnight rates up front.

Quick decision guide

  • Age 60+, limited assets, high care needs: start with CCP.
  • Have a long-term care policy: call the insurer first, then the agency.
  • Veteran or surviving spouse: ask about Aid and Attendance.
  • Otherwise: private pay, possibly with a health plan benefit.

FAQ

Can I use more than one source at once? Often yes. Agencies commonly coordinate several.
